NEW · NOT YET PUSHED TURN 8 · RARITY WITHOUT ZONES

Rarity is what a bird does

No bird belongs to a zone any more. Save for whatever you like, whenever you like — a Barn Owl at three birds is fine, and he moves into the Meadow because that is where you are. Every bird you own is then on its perch every single day; the only empty perch in the app belongs to a seasonal migrant, which is what makes that absence read as travel. What a rarer bird buys is behaviour — more to watch, more that answers you, more that changes with the hour.

8a

The four tiers — rarity is what a bird does

Every bird you own is in the meadow every single day. What a rarer bird buys is not scarcity, it is behaviour: more to watch, more that reacts to you, more that changes with the hour. A common bird is good company; a rare one puts on a show. The only empty perch a kid ever sees belongs to a seasonal migrant, which is exactly what makes that absence read as travel rather than loss.

GOOD COMPANY Common 15–25 One idle and one play loop. Bobs on its perch, wanders to the feeder now and then. The backbone of a young meadow.
HAS A TRICK Uncommon 40–60 A signature move, three or four times a session. The kingfisher dives at the pond, the woodpecker drums the trunk, the swallow loops the whole zone. You learn to wait for it.
PUTS ON A SHOW Rare 90–130 A signature move, a reaction to you, and a time of day that is hers. The kestrel hovers dead still before a stoop. Tap her and she answers instead of just hopping. Nearby birds scatter when she lands, and she hunts at dusk.
ONE OF A KIND Legendary egg only All of the above, plus a moment no other bird has. The crane dances — a full ten-second display, a few times a week, that stops the rest of the meadow to watch. Hatched and named by the kid.
  • Nobody is ever missing. Every owned bird is on its perch every day, at every hour. There is no roll, no chance, nothing to check back for.
  • Seasonal migrants are the single exception — and they leave with the whole ceremony in 7d, so an empty perch always has a story attached to it.
  • Behaviour is the thing being bought, so the store card names the tier and the signature move. The guide never does — the book is about the real species, and what your bird gets up to belongs to the meadow.
  • Rare behaviour never demands attention. No badge, no notification, no “you missed it”. The kestrel hovers whether or not anyone is watching, which is the point.
  • Cost per tier for animation: commons reuse the shared idle and play loops; uncommons add one bespoke move each; rares add a move, a tap response and a dusk variant; the legendary adds a set piece. That is the real budget line behind the price ladder.

NEW · NOT YET PUSHED TURN 7 · PANNING · PERCHES · HATCHING · LEAVING ROUGH SKETCH · NOT FINAL ART

Panning to the frontier, and two ceremonies

Panning comes back: the earned zones are one continuous landscape a kid can drag through freely, and the hedge is a hard wall at the far edge of the last one they have found. Below that: many more places for birds to perch and play, the hatching ceremony beat by beat, and the leaving ceremony that closes each season.

7a

Pan as far as you have flown

The world scrolls, but it ends where you do. Earned zones join edge to edge with a continuous ground line; the frontier is a hedge you can pan up to and not past. Nothing beyond it is drawn, dimmed or hinted at — the drag simply stops with a soft 120ms rubber-band, the way the end of a photo roll does.

  • Chips still work and now animate as a 600ms pan rather than a cut — tapping “Pond” travels there so the geography stays true.

7b

Somewhere to perch, something to do

Each zone carries twelve or more perches instead of open air: real objects a bird sits on, and four play spots a bird uses on its own. A bird's saved slot is now a named perch rather than an x/y coordinate, which makes placement readable and lets two birds share a branch.

ROUGH SKETCH · PERCH INVENTORY FOR ONE ZONE HIGH BRANCH ×2 LOW BRANCH ×2 TRUNK HOLLOW REEDS ×2 PLAY · SHALLOWS PLAY · FEEDER NEST BOX PLAY · BIRD BATH PLAY · DUST PATCH FENCE POST ×2
  • Perches per zone: 2 high branch, 2 low branch, 1 trunk hollow, 2 reeds, 1 nest box, 2 fence posts, 1 log, plus the ground — fourteen slots for a cap of seven birds, so the meadow never looks like assigned seating.
  • Four play spots per zone in Sun on the map above: the shallows, the feeder, the bird bath, the dust patch. A bird drifts to one every few minutes, uses it for 6–10s, and returns to its perch.
  • Play loops (tier 1, ambient): splash at the shallows 8s, peck at the feeder 6s, shake at the bath 10s, ruffle in the dust 7s. One bird at a time per spot; a second bird waits on its perch.
  • Species pick their own kind of perch — waders take reeds and shallows, perchers take branches and posts, the owl takes the hollow. That is what makes a full meadow look right rather than random.
  • Tapping a bird at play still opens its entry; it finishes the loop first, so nothing is ever cut off mid-splash.

7d

The leaving, and the coming back

At the turn of a season the visiting birds migrate. This is the only moment the app removes something a child earned, so it is handled as a goodbye with a promise rather than an expiry: they are warned, they get to watch, the entry stays in the guide, and the return is a real event months later.

  • A bird away on migration still counts toward zone unlocks and the flock total. Nothing regresses.

  • Parent FAQ needs a line on this: birds leaving is seasonal and temporary, and no bird is ever lost.

Open for you

  • Preset names: every ordinary bird now arrives already named, drawn at random from six curated names per species (Blue Jay → Bluebell, Sky, Patch, Acorn, Scout, Jibby). Do you want a parent-side way to reroll one, or is the given name simply the bird's name?
  • 7a restores panning and so retires 6a's cross-fade; the four scenes must now share one horizon line, which is a note for the illustrator brief.
  • Play spots add four small looping animations per zone (16 total). If that is too much, the bath and the dust patch are the two to cut.
  • 7d assumes autumn is the only departure. Spring arrivals of the summer visitors would then be silent — worth giving them the same arrival flight.

Four scenes, a guidebook, and a shop counter

The widening clearing is gone. There are exactly four scenes and the only time the view changes is the moment a zone opens — that is one animation, played once, per zone. A zone you have not earned cannot be entered, panned to, or peeked at; the world simply ends at a hedge with a sign on it. Below: the revised zone model, the Field Guide up close, the store, all four seasons, and every animation in the Meadow with its timing.

Tapping a bird in the scene opens its entry, and nothing else.
