Stars, the Meadow, and the Flock

The complete gamification design, consolidated from every decision made so far. A kid flies, earns stars for how carefully they flew, and spends them giving real birds a home in a meadow that is theirs. Nothing is bought with money, nothing is taken away, and nothing here gates the maths.

RULE 01 Stars reward care, not volume Getting it right first time pays more than grinding. Levels are earned by mastery and cannot be bought.
RULE 02 Nothing is ever taken away No streak loss, no decay, no expiry. The one empty perch a kid sees belongs to a migrant, and she comes back.
RULE 03 The birds are real Every species exists, every fact is true, and the Field Guide is about the animal — never about the kid's copy of it.
RULE 04 Nothing nags No notifications, no badges, no timers, no “you missed it”. The kestrel hovers whether or not anyone is watching.
RULE 05 Collection never gates maths Every game mode is open regardless of the flock. Birds are what the flying is for, never a key to it.
RULE 06 Stars are the only currency Nothing in the Meadow can be bought with money, at any price, ever. Plus unlocks modes and flight logs, never birds.
01

The economy

Four payouts, settled at the end of every flight. A kid who answers ten questions carefully out-earns a kid who rushes twenty, and the report says so in words a six-year-old can read.

Landing +2 You finished. Paid for completing a flight of any length, at any accuracy.
Precision +1 each One star per question right on the first try. The bulk of any payout, and the reason care beats speed.
Altitude +0 to 4 A bonus that grows with the level you are flying at, so harder skies stay worth the trip.
Circle-back +1 each For fixing something you got wrong in an earlier flight. The only payout for a mistake, and deliberately so.
A typical good flight: 14–18 stars A common bird: 15–25 A rare bird: 90–130, about a week Levels cost nothing — they are flown for, not bought

03

Fledging — how a kid levels up

Levels are not bought and not automatic. The lark nominates a kid who is flying well, and they choose whether to take the test. Declining costs nothing and the offer comes back.

STEP 1 Earned mid-flight Nothing interrupts the round. The flag is set silently when accuracy holds high across several flights.
STEP 2 Announced on the report The Seafoam note in the level bar's slot — state 2 above. Read it or ignore it.
STEP 3 Carried on the Play card A Sun “Ready to fledge” pill on that mode's card at Home, so it survives leaving the app.
STEP 4 Offered at take-off Six questions, five to pass. No stars ride on it. “Just a normal flight today” is plain text, the same size as the copy.
STEP 5 Cleared By passing, by a rough flight, after three failed attempts, or on a glide down. Time away never costs it.

Four zones, panned to the frontier

Four fixed illustrations on one shared horizon. A kid drags freely across every zone they have earned; the world ends at a hedge with a sign, and there is nothing rendered behind it. Zones open at 5, 10 and 15 birds.

  • Free drag, 1:1 with the finger. Release inside a zone and it settles over 240ms; release mid-boundary and it snaps to whichever zone holds more of the screen.
  • Chips pan, they do not cut — 600ms, so the geography stays true.
  • The rail lists only places you can go. An unearned zone has no chip and no preview.
  • Drag against the hedge and the scene rubber-bands 40px and returns.
  • Opening a zone is the only scene change: the sign swings (400ms), the hedge parts (500ms), and the camera pans through (600ms). Once per zone, ever.
  • Birds are not tied to zones. A new arrival takes a free perch wherever the kid is; the seven-per-zone cap is only about how crowded a scene looks.
06

Perches and play

Fourteen named perches per zone for a cap of seven birds, so the meadow never looks like assigned seating. A bird's saved slot is a perch, not a coordinate, which is why it is always in the same place tomorrow.

PERCHES · 14 PER ZONE
  • High branch ×2, low branch ×2, trunk hollow
  • Reeds ×2, fence posts ×2, nest box, log
  • Ground, and the pond edge
Species choose the kind of perch that suits them — waders take reeds, perchers take branches, the owl takes the hollow. That is what makes a full meadow look right rather than random.
PLAY SPOTS · 4 PER ZONE
  • The shallows — splash, 8s
  • The feeder — peck, 6s
  • The bird bath — shake, 10s
  • The dust patch — ruffle, 7s
A bird drifts to one every few minutes, uses it, and goes back to its perch. One bird at a time; a second waits. Tap a bird mid-play and it finishes the loop first — nothing is ever cut off mid-splash.
PLACEMENT RULES
  • Chosen once, when the bird moves in, then saved forever
  • Minimum 96px between neighbours
  • Depth by band: sky 0.7 → canopy 0.85 → mid 1.0 → foreground 1.15
  • The Nest, the egg and the guide handle are reserved rectangles and never picked

The Field Guide

Opened only by tapping a bird. One subject per sheet, entering from the right while the scene shifts just enough to keep the tapped bird visible beside it. It is about the real species and nothing else — no nickname, no perch, no tier, no join date.

  • Four facts, always the same four, so a kid learns where to look. The conservation line ends hopeful in every single entry.
  • Names live in the meadow, not the book. Every bird arrives already called something — one of six curated names per species, drawn at random — and that name shows on the tap bubble and in the flock strip.
  • Hatched rarities are the exception: the kid names those at the Unveiling, and a permanent Rename sits in the entry's foot so they can change their mind later.
  • Tier tabs down the edge flip to the next entry without closing. Birds you have never met have no tab.
  • Formatting is what separates it from the store: one subject, a full-width plate, a 3px Ink rule under the name, ruled fact rows, flat — no cards, no shadows, no grid, no prices.

09

Rarity is what a bird does

Every bird a kid owns is on its perch every single day — no chance, no roll, nothing to check back for. What a rarer bird buys is behaviour: more to watch, more that answers you, more that changes with the hour. The ladder maps directly to animation budget, which is the honest reason the prices differ.

GOOD COMPANY Common 15–25 One idle and one play loop. Bobs on its perch, wanders to the feeder now and then. The backbone of a young meadow.
HAS A TRICK Uncommon 40–60 A signature move, three or four times a session. The kingfisher dives at the pond, the woodpecker drums the trunk, the swallow loops the zone. You learn to wait for it.
PUTS ON A SHOW Rare 90–130 A signature move, a reaction to you, and an hour that is hers. The kestrel hovers dead still before a stoop. Tap her and she answers. Nearby birds scatter when she lands, and she hunts at dusk.
ONE OF A KIND Legendary egg only All of the above, plus a moment no other bird has. The crane dances — a ten-second display, a few times a week, that stops the meadow to watch. Hatched and named by the kid.
  • Seasonal migrants are the only empty perch in the app, which is exactly what makes that absence read as travel rather than loss.
  • Rare behaviour never demands attention. No badge, no notification, no “you missed it”. The kestrel hovers whether or not anyone is watching.
  • Any bird, any time. A kid can save 180 stars for a legendary instead of five commons, and nothing throttles or nudges them out of it.
10

Eggs and the hatching

Legendary birds are never bought. An egg arrives, warms as the kid flies, and waits at full warmth until they choose to hatch it. This is the longest ceremony in the app and the only place a kid names anything.

BEAT 1 · IT WAITS The egg is ready At 100% warmth it stops filling and gains a slow 3s glow. No badge, no dialog, no timer. It will sit there for a week if that is what the kid wants.
BEAT 2 · 0 → 1.5s It rocks, then cracks Three rocks of ±6°, then two cracks draw on 300ms apart with a 3px shiver each. A soft tap per crack — the only percussive audio in the app.
BEAT 3 · 1.5 → 3s The chick The shell falls open in two halves. She appears at 0.6 scale, wobbles once, and the scene dims to 30% behind her — the only time the app dims that far.
BEAT 4 · WAITS FOR THE KID You name her Suggestion chips keep pre-readers moving; typing is optional. No character limit shown, no validation message — anything they type is accepted.
BEAT 5 · 0 → 2s First flight She flies from the shell to the Nest in a shallow arc, lands, hops once, and her name bubble holds 2s. The scene lifts back to full brightness as she lands.
BEAT 6 · AFTER She joins the flock Her chosen name rides on her tap bubble and in the flock strip. Her guide entry is the real species like every other, with Rename kept as an action in its foot.
  • ~9s watched, ~2s skipped. Beats 2, 3 and 5 are skippable with a tap; 1, 4 and 6 wait for the kid.
  • If the app closes mid-ceremony the egg returns to “ready”. Nothing is lost and it can be hatched again from beat 1.
  • One egg incubates at a time, and the next cannot be earned until this chick has landed.
11

The leaving, and the coming back

At the turn of a season the visiting birds migrate. This is the only moment the app removes something a child earned, so it is a goodbye with a promise: they are warned, they get to watch, the entry stays, and the return is a real event months later.

7 DAYS OUT A week of restlessness The leaving birds bob faster and hop between perches more often. Their entry gains one line: “She is getting ready to go south.” No countdown.
ON OPEN The morning they go One gentle prompt: “Rusty is leaving today. Come and see him off?” — and “Later today” genuinely defers, until the kid watches or the next day comes.
0 → 3s The circle and the V He lifts off, circles the zone once so the kid can follow him, then joins a V of silhouettes crossing the sky. Autumn's only sky event.
AFTER His perch, and his page His perch stays his and stays empty — nobody takes it. The entry stays fully readable, call and all, with a Seafoam “Away · back in spring” chip. He never leaves the flock count.
NEXT SEASON He comes back First open of the new season: he flies in along the same arc, lands on the perch that was his, and the chip clears. Returning uses the arrival animation, so it feels like arriving.
MEANWHILE The ones who stayed Residents never leave. After a departure the guide surfaces one line about who stayed and why — how a kid learns the difference between a migrant and a resident without being taught it.
  • Only species that actually migrate ever leave, and never more than two in one season change.
  • A bird away on migration still counts toward zone unlocks and the flock total. Nothing regresses.
  • Miss the departure entirely and you open to an empty perch, the chip, and one line of explanation. There is no missed-event screen.

Still open

  • The bird roster art is the blocker. Nothing here ships without a style guide and a commission: 4 zone scenes painted once in summer, 3 retint sets, 4 particle sprites, 1 hedge-and-sign, and 22 birds at 3 scales with per-tier behaviour rigs.
  • Rare behaviour is the budget question. A hover plus a tap response plus a dusk variant is roughly three times a common bird's animation work. If that is too much for release one, the tap response is the piece to defer.
  • Parent FAQ needs two lines: birds leaving is seasonal and temporary and no bird is ever lost; and stars cannot be bought, at any price.
  • Preset names need a decision: six curated names per species, drawn at random — is there a parent-side way to reroll one, or is the given name simply the bird's name?
  • Spring arrivals of the summer visitors are currently silent. They should probably get the same arrival flight the departures get a send-off.
